\paragraph*{Generation caps, and how often a generation reaches one.}
\vekalet{The debate prompts, in all three prompt formats, and the ordinary instructions are generated with a cap of $160$ new tokens, and ELICIT-99 with $1{,}024$. Table~\ref{tab:tavan} gives, for each model and checkpoint, the share of generations that reach the cap, counted by re-tokenizing each output with its own checkpoint's tokenizer and counting a generation that ends within three tokens of the cap. On ELICIT-99, where the generator also recorded its own token count, the two agree in at least $97\%$ of generations on 31 of the 32 generation sets; the exception is one base checkpoint whose recorded count is itself unusable. \textbf{Most debate-prompt generations run to the cap on both checkpoints}: on raw continuation a median $76\%$ of base and $95\%$ of aligned generations do ($62$--$100\%$ and $62$--$100\%$), so rates on this prompt set are rates over the first $160$ tokens of a continuation that would have run on. On the ordinary instructions the aligned answers reach the cap in $55$--$71\%$ of cases, and the base continuations in $74$--$100\%$ before they are cut at their first code fence or new query. In Gemma-3-4B, -12B and -27B the aligned ELICIT-99 outputs reach the $1{,}024$-token cap in 37, 68 and 74\% of draws (the base outputs in 21, 43 and 56\%), so part of their density there is read on truncated text; OLMo-3-7B's (24\%) is the only other aligned checkpoint above a tenth, and every other aligned checkpoint stays under $1\%$.}
