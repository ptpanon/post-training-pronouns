The spread of \S\ref{sec:deperson} is read here against quantities alignment does manage, on the same chat-template cells after the same four-quadrant filter and with the same measure, the ratio of the largest model value to the smallest across the sixteen models. Second-person density widens from $2.5\times$ across the bases to $20.5\times$ across the aligned checkpoints, while the share of list and code lines narrows from $18.8\times$ to $4.2\times$ and response length from $1.44\times$ to $1.30\times$: alignment draws together the quantities it manages and pulls this one apart. Read with the default system line absent on every model and without the four-quadrant filter, the aligned band spans $19.8\times$, and $18.6\times$ with echo and turn-marker rows dropped, so the spread is not those two inputs. A ratio of extremes is unstable where a minimum nears zero --- the smallest base share of list and code lines is $0.012$ --- and refusal rate, zero in eight aligned models and in one base, cannot be read this way at all. The comparison is descriptive and was not pre-registered; it gives a reference class, not a null.
