\paragraph*{The first-person fall on the prefix conditions that carry no first person.}
\vekalet{Twelve of the $16$ prefix conditions carry first-person forms of their own, so a base checkpoint could copy them. Four carry none (the arousal conditions, one- and four-utterance, in both directions), and two of these carry no second person either. We re-read the first-person change on those four alone (Table~\ref{tab:birincisiz}), on the original sampling seed, with the prompt-clustered interval of the full panel ($1{,}000$ draws). The same loop reproduces the full panel's first-person change in all $16$ models, to within $10^{-9}$, before any subset is read. \textbf{On the four conditions the first person falls in $15$ of $16$ models}, counted directly or as derived, and Qwen2.5-1.5B is the exception (interval covering zero). On the two conditions that carry neither person the first person falls in $15$ and rises in $1$, and the second person falls in $10$, rises in $1$ and is unclear in $5$. The four conditions hold a quarter of the data and the two an eighth, so intervals are wider and an unclear reading is a loss of power, not a finding of no change. The reading is descriptive: its rule was committed before the count, with no bar.}
