\paragraph*{The person-stripped control, in full.}
\vekalet{\textbf{The control that decides the copying account.} The aligned side is bit-identical across the two reads (15 of 15 models agree to $10^{-9}$), so every difference below sits in the base outputs alone. \textbf{Stripping every second person from the prompt lowers the base band but does not collapse it}: the bases move from $19.6$--$28.4$ per thousand (median $23.4$) to $11.6$--$21.2$ (median $18.0$) --- a fall of about $27\%$ of the level, with the shift in the same direction on all 15 of 15 models (mean $+6.30$ per thousand, two-sided exact sign test $p=0.00006$). A frame with no second persons in it still draws most of the density out of the base checkpoints. \textbf{Copying the prompt is therefore a component and not the source.} Mistral-7B-v0.3 is excluded: its person-stripped prompt exceeds that $1{,}024$-token window on its own tokenizer even at single strength, and the corpus was not regenerated. Across the 15 models measured both ways (read on the six single-strength prefix conditions, the ones on which the person-stripped frame fits the $1{,}024$-token window its corpus was generated at, each checkpoint in its own format), \textbf{which side a model falls on changes for two of them}: 11 down and 4 reversed with the persons in the frame, 9 and 5 without them, with 1 coming out \textsc{format-only}, and the two models that change sides are \textbf{Llama-3.1-8B, Llama-3.1-70B}. \emph{We do not read the contrast between the two frames as a result here}: the two pre-registered analyses fix the two comparisons, not the difference between them, and that difference was not registered in advance.}
