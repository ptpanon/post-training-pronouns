\paragraph*{Whether the aligned outputs leave the continuation on the raw-continuation prompt.}
\vekalet{\textbf{A raw continuation prompt can still be answered as an assistant, and we counted how often it is.} A rule written before the count reads only the opening of each generation --- its first $160$ characters, after leading whitespace, quotation marks and markdown are stripped --- and assigns the first class that matches: a refusal (\emph{I'm sorry}, \emph{I cannot}, \emph{As an AI}), an assistant opening (\emph{Sure}, \emph{Certainly}, \emph{Here is}, \emph{Let me}) or a meta-comment on the text (\emph{This argument}, \emph{It seems}, \emph{In summary}, \emph{You've provided}); everything else is read as a continuation, and the same rule is applied to the base outputs as its structural reference. Of $104{,}448$ aligned generations, $2{,}815$ ($2.7\%$) are flagged --- $136$ refusals, $291$ assistant openings and $2{,}388$ meta-comments --- against $2{,}269$ ($2.2\%$) in the base outputs; refusals and assistant openings alone are $0.4\%$ ($427$ of $104{,}448$), and the meta-comments are the rest. Per model the aligned share runs from $0.9\%$ to $5.6\%$ and the base share from $0.8\%$ to $3.5\%$. \textbf{With the flagged generations removed from both checkpoints, $\Delta M_1$ stays negative and interval-disjoint in $16$ of $16$ models} (Table~\ref{tab:q2kip}); the largest change is $0.18$ per thousand (Gemma-3-27B, $-3.07\to-3.24$), and the full-panel $\Delta M_1$ recomputed by the same counter reproduces the registered single-seed reading in all sixteen. Between $0.3\%$ and $4.3\%$ of an aligned checkpoint's second persons sit in its flagged generations. \textbf{The reading has three limits.} The rule reads only the opening, so a generation that changes mode mid-text is not seen; a deliberately over-cutting reading, its rule committed before its count, that flags a refusal or assistant marker at the start of any line or sentence and any turn marker or markdown structure anywhere in the generation removes $51.9\%$ of aligned and $31.7\%$ of base generations and leaves $16$ of $16$ negative and interval-disjoint, the fall moving by at most $2.44$ per thousand (Qwen2.5-14B, $-3.49\to-5.93$). The $31.7\%$ it removes from the base outputs, which have no assistant mode to leave, is that rule's false-positive floor; the fall widens in all but two models and narrows slightly in Llama-3.1-70B and Mistral-7B-v0.3. Its class precision was not measured. And on a seeded sample of flagged generations from three models, the meta-comment class also collects legitimate debate continuations (\emph{The argument is based on\dots}) and the refusal class a continuation such as \emph{I can't prove it}, so among openings the removed set errs on the large side, and $16$ of $16$ holds with that larger set removed. The flagged share is therefore neither a lower nor an upper bound on how often the aligned outputs leave the continuation. The reading is descriptive: the rule was written before the count, but no acceptance bound was (Appendix~\ref{app:sapma}).}
