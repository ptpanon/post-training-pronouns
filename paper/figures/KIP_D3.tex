\paragraph*{Between and within opening modes, by a judge.}
\vekalet{Because the rule's \emph{continue} class is imprecise, a judge also labelled the chat-template outputs: Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct at temperature 0, a Qwen2.5 model like six of the sixteen, read $3{,}105$ aligned outputs drawn by opening class and marked each as a reply to the user turn, a continuation of it, or neither; its labels are weighted back to each model's opening classes. We split the variance of $\ln M_1$ across the aligned outputs into a between-mode and a within-mode part over the model-by-mode cells that hold at least $5\%$ of a model's outputs (20 of 32; the other 12, all continuations, fall below that share or were not sampled). $85\%$ of the variance lies within modes. Replies carry $96\%$ of the weight and alone spread with a geometric standard deviation of $2.10$, against $2.33$ over these cells and $2.35$ over all generations (\S\ref{sec:deperson}). The spread under the template is therefore present among replies, not mainly a difference between models that answer and models that continue. On well-formed input, with the genre fixed by a header on raw continuation (Appendix~\ref{app:D2}), the aligned checkpoints span $2.99\times$ (geometric SD $1.33$) against $2.28\times$ ($1.26$) for the bases, and the aligned range exceeds $3\times$ in two of the four genres, forum posts ($6.84\times$, bases $2.95\times$) and how-to guides ($4.06\times$, bases $1.82\times$). Both readings are descriptive, their paths committed before the count. Against one human labeller on $100$ outputs drawn by model and checkpoint, the judge's label agrees on $70$ (raw agreement $0.70$, model-clustered $95\%$ interval $[0.60, 0.78]$; Cohen's $\kappa=0.31$, chance agreement $0.57$). The two mode labels match for $44$ of $49$ aligned-checkpoint generations but for $26$ of $51$ base-checkpoint generations; most mismatches are texts the labeller read as continuations and the judge called replies ($16$, $14$ from base checkpoints). That is below the $0.80$ we fixed before comparing. A second label judge (Claude Sonnet~5, same model family as the assistant) was tried after the first missed the bar; its agreement on the same 100 items is $78/100$ ($\kappa=0.53$, chance agreement $0.53$; on those same outputs it matches the labeller $46$ times among aligned checkpoints and $32$ among bases); its labels are used only where that bar is met. Both judges were compared with the labeller on these same 100 outputs, so the second was chosen on them. It does not meet the bar either, so this decomposition stays here, labelled by the open-weight cross-check judge, and does not enter the main text. Within outputs the judge labels as continuations, the template split cannot be read: no model has $20\%$ continuations in both checkpoints, the share its rule requires (the other aligned checkpoints reach at most $14\%$; T\"ulu-3-8B reaches $26\%$ against $14\%$ for its base). The seven models whose rate rises under the template reply to the prompt in $84\%$ to $98\%$ of their aligned outputs, the other nine in $74\%$ to $100\%$. Replying rather than continuing does not separate the risers from the rest, and the label does not say whether a reply comments on the prompt as a prompt.}
