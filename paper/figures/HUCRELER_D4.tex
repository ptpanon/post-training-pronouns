\paragraph*{Four cells on the ordinary instructions (\S\ref{sec:deperson}).}
\vekalet{The reading above pairs each aligned model in its own chat template with its base in the URIAL frame, so the two checkpoints differ in prompt as well as in weights. Three further cells hold the string fixed, strip it of persons, or both (Table~\ref{tab:cells}). In \textbf{B1} (the reading above) the checkpoints are in different formats. In \textbf{K1} both checkpoints read the identical URIAL string, the released \texttt{inst\_1k} prompt at $K=3$, so the aligned model reads a format it was not trained on. In \textbf{K2} the aligned outputs are unchanged and the base reads a version of that frame stripped of first- and second-person forms. In \textbf{K3} both checkpoints read that person-stripped string. Every cell uses the same $300$ instructions, four sampling seeds, degeneracy shelf, instruction-clustered intervals ($1{,}000$ draws) and within-instruction paired placebo. Every URIAL-format output is cut at its first code fence or new query line, and the first-person count is direct, with a capitalised \emph{US} not counted. The reading rule for K1 and K2 and the registration of K3 were committed before any of their generations was counted, and all four cells are descriptive, with no bar. \textbf{In its own format the aligned model says ``you'' less in all 16 models, and against a base on the person-stripped frame in all 16.} On an identical string the sign splits: $11/3/2$ down, up and unclear on the standard frame and $6/7/3$ on the person-stripped one, where Gemma-3-27B, Gemma-3-12B, Gemma-3-4B, Llama-3.1-8B, Llama-3.1-70B, Qwen2.5-3B and OLMo-2-32B rise. ``I'' holds better: it falls in $9$ models on the standard string and $10$ on the person-stripped one. \textbf{On prose lines alone the instruction reading is unchanged}: in B1 ``you'' falls in 16 of 16 models per token and 16 per sentence, and in K2 in 16 and 16; ``I'' falls in 12 and 12 in B1 and in 12 and 11 in K2. On prose lines alone, with list, numbered and heading lines dropped (a median $54\%$ of aligned lines against $44\%$ of base lines in K3), ``you'' falls in $7$ and rises in $8$ per token, and falls in $5$ and rises in $8$ per sentence, while ``I'' falls in $7$ per token and $8$ per sentence and rises in at most $1$. \textbf{Cutting the aligned outputs at their first code fence as well}, as the base outputs are cut, changes at most $1.1\%$ of aligned rows (T\"ulu-3-8B), and ``you'' still falls in $16$ of $16$ models and ``I'' in $12$. The aligned model is therefore read in its own format for the claim about ``you'' on instructions, and the identical-string cells are reported as a limit of that claim (Limitations).}
